How to compare construction quotations without being overcharged

The lowest headline rate is rarely the cheapest project. Compare scope, specifications and exclusions before you decide which quotation is actually lower.

Updated 20 November 2026 · By Vanilla & Somethin' · Architecture & Interior Design, Mangalore

Use the comparison toolGet a quotation reviewed

Why headline rates mislead

Quotations for the same house can differ for reasons that have little to do with how well the work is priced. Start with the measurement basis. One contractor may quote per sq ft on built-up area, another on plinth area and a third on carpet area. These are different numbers, and a rate on one basis cannot be compared directly with a rate on another.

Next, check what is included. Two quotes can carry the same headline rate but cover different work. Specification grades matter too: a rate built on standard fittings and a rate built on premium fittings describe different houses. Finally, look at how extras are priced. Some contractors leave items out of the base rate and bill them later as variations.

Consider a simple example, with illustrative numbers only. Quotation A offers ₹2,000 per sq ft and Quotation B offers ₹2,300 per sq ft. On 1,500 sq ft of built-up area, that is ₹30 lakh against ₹34.5 lakh, a difference of ₹4.5 lakh in favour of A.

Now suppose Quotation A excludes a compound wall, a sump, electrical fittings and waterproofing, and those items add up to more than ₹4.5 lakh on your site. Quotation B, which includes them, may then be the cheaper project. The headline rate pointed one way and the complete scope points the other.

Note: the rates and totals above are illustrative. They are not a quotation from any contractor or from our studio.

Before you ask for quotations

Most comparison problems start before any quote arrives. If each contractor works from different information, you will receive prices for different houses. Before you send out your enquiry:

  • Give every contractor the same drawings, specification schedule and scope list.
  • Fix the measurement basis in writing, for example built-up area across all floors, and ask every contractor to quote on that basis.
  • Ask for trade-wise breakdowns covering structure, masonry, finishes, electrical, plumbing and external works, rather than one lump sum.
  • State what is outside the scope, such as land, approvals, the compound wall and loose furniture, so that exclusions are explicit from the start.

If you do not yet have drawings or a specification schedule, an architect or designer can prepare them. Structural decisions, including foundation assumptions, should be confirmed by a structural engineer, and a quantity surveyor can check quantities before you rely on them.

Red flags in a quotation

Treat any of the following as a reason to ask questions before you compare totals:

  1. A single lump sum with no breakdown.
  2. “As per site condition” written against major items such as foundation or excavation.
  3. Brands listed without grades or model numbers.
  4. Provisional sums with no cap.
  5. No list of exclusions.
  6. Payments front-loaded, with large amounts due before the work they pay for is done.
  7. No written process for approving changes.

A red flag does not prove that a contractor is poor value. Each one, however, leaves room for a final cost well above the quoted figure.

How to handle changes and extras

Changes are where many budgets drift. Agree a rule before work starts: no change proceeds until you have approved a written change request. Each request should state the scope change, the cost impact and the time impact, and it should be approved before the work is carried out.

Keep a simple change log with the date, a description, the cost, the time impact and the approval status of each change. Review it against the contract at every payment stage. Provisional sums should be converted into fixed amounts through the same written process.

Frequently asked questions

Should I always pick the lowest quote?

No. The lowest headline rate is often the quotation that leaves the most out. Check scope, specification and exclusions first. If one quote is clearly lower, ask that contractor to explain every gap before you rule the others out.

How many quotations should I get?

Three comparable quotations is common practice. The quality of the comparison matters more than the number, so three quotes on the same drawings and scope are more useful than six built on different assumptions.

Is GST included in a per sq ft rate?

Ask directly. Many quotes do not state it clearly, so confirm in writing whether GST is included in the rate and how it is applied to each payment.

What is a provisional sum?

A provisional sum is an allowance for an item whose final cost is not yet fixed, such as a fitting or a specialist package. It should carry a stated cap and a clear rule for how any difference is settled.

Can an architect review quotations for me?

Yes. An architect can review quotations from a scope and specification view, checking whether the items compared are genuinely the same. This does not replace a quantity surveyor or a contractor's own pricing.
