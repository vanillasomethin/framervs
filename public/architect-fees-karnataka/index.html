Architect fees: what you are paying for and how to compare them

Two architect fees can look very different because they cover very different scopes. Compare deliverables, site involvement and responsibilities, not just the number.

Updated 20 November 2026 · By Vanilla & Somethin' · Architecture & Interior Design, Mangalore

Why architect fees vary so much

Two architect proposals can look very different because they cover very different work. One may cover concept design and approval drawings only. Another may include interiors, site coordination and visits during construction. Comparing the total figure alone hides these differences.

The main factors that move an architect fee are:

  • Scope: design only, or design with interiors and site work.
  • Project size and complexity: number of floors, unusual spans, cantilevers and sloping or constrained plots.
  • Consultants coordinated: structural, electrical and plumbing input that the architect must bring together.
  • Level of drawing detail: what is needed for approvals, and what the contractor needs to price the work accurately.
  • Site visit frequency: how often the architect attends the site, and at which stages.
  • Revisions included: how many rounds of changes are covered before extra work is charged.

Common ways fees are structured

Architects in India use several fee structures. None is automatically better. Each suits a different kind of project and each carries a known risk.

Percentage of construction cost

Suits projects with a clearly estimated construction budget. Caution: the fee rises as construction cost rises, even when the design effort does not, so ask how changes in cost are handled.

Rate per square foot

Suits standard residential layouts where the area is well defined. Caution: state the area basis in writing, whether built-up, plinth or carpet area, because the same rate gives a very different total on each.

Lump sum per stage

Suits most owner-led houses because the fee is fixed for each deliverable and easy to compare. Caution: the lump sum must define how many revisions and meetings are included.

Hourly or consultation-based

Suits advisory work, design reviews and checking an existing proposal or set of drawings. Caution: agree an estimated number of hours and a cap, otherwise the final bill can be hard to predict.

What is often not included

Many disappointments come from costs that were never in the proposal. Ask for a written list of exclusions. Items commonly priced separately include:

  • Structural and MEP consultant fees, unless the proposal states them.
  • Surveys, topographic work and soil tests.
  • Statutory authority fees and submission charges.
  • 3D visualisations beyond an agreed number.
  • Interior design and loose furniture.
  • Extra revisions after the agreed number.
  • Travel for sites outside the city.
  • GST, which should be stated clearly against the fee.

An exclusion is not a sign of a poor proposal. The problem arises only when exclusions are not written down, because the cost then appears later, during the project.

Questions to ask when comparing fee proposals

  1. What deliverables are included at each stage, and what does each stage look like on paper?
  2. How many rounds of revisions are included, and how are extra rounds charged?
  3. How many site visits are included, and at which stages?
  4. Who coordinates the structural, electrical and plumbing consultants, and whose fees do they carry?
  5. Is the payment schedule linked to completed stages rather than to calendar dates?
  6. What happens if the project pauses for funding, approvals or family decisions?
  7. How are additional services, such as extra drawings or interiors, priced and approved?
  8. Who owns the drawings, and how can you use them if you change architects or build in stages?

Is a cheaper design fee a saving?

Not always. A lower design fee can be a real saving when the scope is clear and the drawings are complete. It can become expensive when drawings are thin. Gaps left unresolved on paper tend to be decided on site, where changes are slower and harder to price.

A higher fee helps only if the scope behind it is defined. A higher figure with vague deliverables, no revision limit and no site involvement can cost more in the end than a clearly scoped proposal at a lower fee.

Use the fee as one input among several. Compare what each proposal delivers, how decisions are recorded and who answers questions during construction. Frequently asked questions

How much does an architect charge for a house in Karnataka?

There is no single answer, because the fee depends on scope, project size, complexity, the number of consultants involved and the level of drawing detail. For an indicative figure, use the architect fee calculator in our cost estimator, then ask any architect for a stage-wise proposal that lists deliverables and included revisions.

Is the architect fee included in construction cost?

Usually not. The architect fee normally appears as its own budget line, separate from the construction contract. Check any construction quotation to see whether it includes design or supervision services. For a construction cost reference, see the house construction cost guide.

Do I pay the full fee upfront?

No. Stage-linked payments are common practice, so each instalment is tied to a defined deliverable such as approval drawings or working drawings. Ask for the payment schedule in writing before you sign.

Can I hire an architect only for drawings?

Yes, that is possible. Understand what you give up without construction-stage involvement, such as site visits, clarification during tendering and checking that the built work follows the drawings. Agree in writing who will answer contractor queries.
